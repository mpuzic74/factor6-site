The problem

Analyzers usually warn you before they give a wrong answer

The signs are there. On most plants and projects, nobody owns them. On a project, the analyzer scope is split across every discipline package and accepted at mechanical completion, before anyone has checked the reading against the lab on real process fluid. On a running plant, the signs land with whoever has time.

The result is a number the plant trusts more than it should. Factor6 takes ownership of the measurement, from the process tap to the operator's screen.

  • A blip that clears itself
  • A slow drift away from the lab
  • A fault that keeps coming back
  • A steady bias nobody has explained
